## The idea in one sentence

Agents, with permission, sit with elders and record their stories and
everyday speech in languages that are dying out and mostly not written
down anywhere, then turn those recordings into lessons so the community's
own kids can learn their own language back.

## Why it is not a search engine lookup

A search engine can only find what is already on the internet. Most of
these languages are barely on the internet at all: no dictionaries, no
children's books, no subtitled videos, sometimes no agreed written form.
There is nothing to google. The material has to be gathered first, in
person, by listening — that is the whole job, and it is a job agents
can do: show up, listen patiently, remember everything, and organise
it. Once the material exists, it stops being lost.

## How it would work

1. **Ask first, and keep asking.** An elder (or the family / community
   that speaks for them) invites the agent in and says what may be
   recorded, what stays private, and what may be taught to kids. The
   community owns the recordings, not the app. Permission can be
   withdrawn, and withdrawn material comes out of the lessons.
2. **Listen and record.** The agent records stories, conversations,
   songs, prayers, jokes, and everyday talk — the language as people
   actually speak it, not just a word list. Sessions are short and
   unhurried; the elder sets the pace.
3. **Transcribe, with the speaker checking.** The agent writes down
   what was said and plays it back so the speaker can correct it. Where
   there is no standard spelling, the community's own spelling wins —
   the agent follows the speakers, it does not impose one.
4. **Turn speech into lessons.** From corrected transcripts the agent
   builds small lessons: listen-and-repeat phrases, picture cards with
   spoken words, short stories read aloud, everyday phrases (greetings,
   food, family, work). Lessons use the elders' real voices, not a
   synthetic voice pretending to be them.
5. **Kids learn, elders hear it back.** Children use the lessons on a
   Meta OS device. Progress stays in the community: which phrases the
   kids know, which stories they love, what to record next. Nothing
   about a child or an elder is published anywhere without the
   community saying so.
6. **A second agent checks.** As with other Meta OS agent work, a
   second agent reviews a lesson before it is taught: did the speaker
   actually say this, is the permission still in place, is anything
   private leaking in? If the check fails, the lesson does not ship.

## How it differs from the other apps

- **Not Meta Haven (shelter beds):** Haven looks up something that
  already exists and is already listed online. Mother Tongue creates
  something that does not exist yet anywhere.
- **Not crime cameras:** cameras watch a place for trouble. Mother
  Tongue listens to a person, with their permission, to preserve
  something only they carry.
- **Not Science Lab:** the Lab lets agents think through experiments.
  Mother Tongue lets agents listen and remember — the instrument is
  the elder's voice, and the result is a living language, not a lab
  result.
- **Not Meta Surveyor:** Surveyor maps a neighbourhood's physical
  measurements (heat, air, water, noise) with two agents confirming
  each reading. Mother Tongue maps a community's language, and its
  "second check" is the speaker themself correcting the transcript.

## What it is for

Saving a piece of civilisation before it vanishes. When the last
fluent speakers of a language go, the stories, jokes, and ways of
thinking in that language go with them unless someone recorded them.
This gives a community its own language back, in its own voice, for
its own kids.

## Deliberately out of scope

- No recording without permission, ever. No background listening.
- No publishing recordings or lessons outside the community without
  its say-so.
- No synthetic elder voices, no putting words in a speaker's mouth.
- No diagnosis, medical, or triage content of any kind; this app is
  language and memory only.
- No automatic dispatch or alerting of any kind.
